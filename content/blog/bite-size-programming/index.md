[Bite Size Programming](/2017-10-14-introducing-bite-size-programming/) was a writing project about programming, written in small pieces.

I started it after becoming a software engineer because I wanted to combine two things I cared about: writing and programming.

The idea was simple: explain one programming concept at a time, in plain language, without trying to sound like a textbook.

The posts covered topics like the [command line](/2017-10-18-why-programmers-use-command-line-interface/), [Git](/2017-11-03-what-is-git/), [Big O notation](/2017-11-15-algorithm-time-complexity-big-o-notation/), linked lists, [binary search](/2017-11-22-binary-search-explained/), binary search trees, stacks, queues, callbacks, caches, and other fundamentals that beginners tend to encounter with a mix of curiosity and dread.

It was partly a teaching project and partly a learning-in-public project. Writing the explanations forced me to clarify what I thought I understood. If I could not explain a concept simply, I probably did not understand it well enough yet.

It also became a way to invite people into programming without making the field feel more intimidating than it already is.
