Few weeks ago I lamented the crossroads I was at and how difficult it was to decide: to return to a job, or to continue trying to build my own business.

I thought I'd resolved it by deciding to continue in business, but circumstances are changing in my life and I'm beginning to consider taking up a job.

I even have a specific job in mind - a Forward Deployed Engineer at an AI company, ideally a frontier model/agent lab, because it fits my expertise really well.

But now I'm torn again. At first it was because I learned firsthand from talking to people recently about acqui-hiring. That's when you've built a compelling business and a bigger business notices you and pays you a handsome sum of money to buy over you, your team, and possibly your IP.

This sounds really attractive as an orthogonal way to enter the fray of the AI race. Build something with AI solving a problem in a very specific vertical, get bought by a large AI player, and then build with AI solving problems at a much larger scale.

But of course this got me thinking: if I could build a moderately successful AI business targeting enterprise customers (what swyx calls "the hardest evals for AI"), maybe I could just continue building out that business. That's a possible alternative path from accepting an acqui-hire offer.

Back to reality, I've got 10 months of experience now after quitting my job to start a business. I have two products, both B2C, and no real business to speak of because there's no revenue yet.

I still have enough money in the bank to fund my runway if I do this solo for several months. This time, in enterprise, I would build a very tightly scoped MVP in a month, and then start demoing it to companies to find lighthouse partners to build it with.

I've already identified and worked out an idea for an AI agent in the hardware procurement space that came from direct experience working with some of them in a previous role. I believe there is a clear pain point to solve there for these companies.

The really hard parts are:

1. deciding to continue after 10 months of little results by burning through more savings
2. convincing myself that I can do the first 10 enterprise sales

The alternative is to find a job where my autonomy and agency as a founder is likely to be respected. Those jobs are rare. And you have to convince them that you're not just hot air but substance. That process is, in my opinion, lossier and less controllable than the process of enterprise software development and sales. There are only a handful of companies that would hire employees and give them real autonomy and respect their agencies, but a ton of enterprises that would be willing to listen to a pitch about software that would drive up revenue and margins by 20-30% through agentic AI workflows.
