YouTube product reviews should be viewed as junk food for your lifestyle.

Let's say you're trying to read more. What do you instinctively do?

I have a friend from university called Shuyan who loves reading. I'll always remember her, among other great qualities, as a reader. She's a voracious reader who reads and never tells people about it. That's when you know when someone does something because it's who they are, not who they want to be.

Meanwhile, adult me has realised how much I've missed out on by not having a reading habit. I want to be more like my friend, to find the love of reading.

So what do I do to read more?

I started by thinking about the best way to read and to read fast and painlessly and smoothly. That's my first thought. My lead domino. Because that's's going to give me the highest chance of success in becoming an avid reader, right?

But what about the other important questions like what genre of books are most likely to appeal to me? Isn't that important too?

Maybe.

And how about taking one step further back and asking, why do I want to be more like Shuyan and read more? What am I feeling like I've been missing out on exactly? Is it intellectual rigour? A better understanding of how people think? A greater appreciation for humanity through history? Or perhaps just the fuzzy feeling of having a productive default habit?

In my mind, those questions are important but secondary to the question of what product I'll actually use to read - [physical books, ebooks, or listening to audiobooks](/physical-or-ebooks-or-listen-to-audiobooks/)? This is already starting off on the wrong foot.

To make things worse, there are many downsides to hearing people review products through their idiosyncratic lenses. And even if they were being objective, like talking about RAM and brightness nits, features are features. We're human, which means our experience of a product is always subjective.

What I've had to **unlearn** lately is to stop listening to YouTube product reviewers tell me how I should think about a product, and just let me first consider what I think would be a great product and find one that's closest to it to buy.

Let's take an example from my life.

I've heard many YouTube reviewers complain about how reading on an iPad is not as comfortable as reading on a Kindle because you can't switch off the screen's backlight. It's blasting blue light into your retina all the time! A Kindle can work without any backlight if the room's bright enough! _Your eyes will thank you!_

But when I recently picked up my iPad to read, I realised how small a problem backlight is to me compared to many other things that I value and gained.

Like the joy of using a library reading app (Libby) designed to get out of the way and put the content first, with no features trying to sell me anything, unlike the Kindle app, which tries to get me to buy new books. Or the joy of being able to read with a sepia coloured background and full coloured images instead of black and white.

It keeps coming back to this, but [do things with a light touch](/2020-07-19-the-magic-of-a-light-touch/). If you want to start reading more, ask not how but just [pick up a book](/2019-11-24-always-make-time-for-books/) and start reading.

If I decide after reading that hey, I want to try out reading it in a different medium, then I would pick up whatever medium (iPhone, iPad, computer) I already have at my disposal and continue reading there.

The magical outcome is that I'd have been reading all this time and products have been mostly absent from my mind.

And when I feel curious to explore a new device/software, I'll go to the store or borrow a friend's and feel it for myself. That way, I'll judge it properly by my own criteria.

I continue to read on my backlit iPad via the Libby app. My retinas are fine.
