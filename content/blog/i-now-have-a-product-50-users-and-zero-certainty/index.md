When I first went solo in mid-June 2025, I pursued the idea of a **YouTube channel** relentlessly.

The idea was a channel dedicated to sharing the real expat stories in Europe. I interviewed 10 of my friends in Berlin, produced most, and published them [on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@NickAngHD).

About one month into it, facing a lukewarm response, I gave up on this idea and moved on to my second.

I found the second idea only after having tackled my first idea. This is a key pattern that shall repeat for the rest of this story.

I realised that editing video interviews is extremely tedious. I was spending anywhere between 8 to 20 (!) hours editing a single 1 hour interview down to a 20 minutes YouTube video. It was crazy and totally unsustainable. By the end of such a day I felt myself completely drained.

So I thought, hey, maybe I could build a tool that helps me edit faster with less pain.

**That idea eventually became TrimScribe**, which I've written about (with a demo video) in [another post](https://nickang.substack.com/p/worklife-update-2-free-life-upgrades). It's a tool that lets you edit your videos based on the transcript rather than the timeline.

I eventually realised after building a working prototype that this only solves part of the tedium of editing videos. So I found myself at a T-junction.

I could continue down the same direction and build out the product, or I could do a hard right and do something else altogether.

At the time, because I really couldn't see myself producing YouTube videos again for a while, I chose to make a hard right.

*How could I maintain my motivation to build out a full product in the interview video editing space if I didn't care about the results?*

So that was the death of idea #2.

---

At around this time, I enrolled myself in a German language course. The government was paying for me as an unemployed permanent resident to attend (amazing, I know!).

The problem: it runs for 4 months. This was the logistics of the course:

- Monday to Friday, 8:30am to 12:45am
- In-person with 20 others

That's 4 hours carved out from my daily work schedule. It was like a death sentence for me as an entrepreneur.

But actually, it became the best thing that happened on my journey so far. Because I started building **something for learning languages** to help me, well, learn a language.

This became idea #3.

Suddenly, my business idea was in lock-step with my personal life. For the first time, there's a sense of unity.

I then built the first version of this app, which I originally called Slang and had joked about being called LinguiMe.

After a couple of weeks, I finally shared it with a few people whom I knew were also learning languages. They were my first EVER group of early testers, a total of 5 people living in Germany, Netherlands, and Spain.

I'll be honest, the feedback from them wasn't positive. Most of them didn't know the concept of flashcard software the way I thought they would.

They didn't understand the flow that seemed super intuitive to me as an Anki user:

1. You see a card in English
2. You try to produce the translated version of that word in your target language
3. You rate yourself on how difficult/easy it was to produce that word correctly
4. The card will be shown to you again based on your rating (spaced repetition)

Blah.

---

What do I do?

I could find a different group of test users, closer to my imagined target audience for this app, and build it for them.

But I didn't do that because one of these test users said something to me, once again changing the course of what I'd do:

"Gut feeling: I understand it's an mvp, but I am not sire that flipping the card and auto-evaluating myself is the best. Can't the app prove if I know that word?"

>

*"Can't the app prove if I know that word?" *

Hmm...

For some reason that made me think of THE language learning app, Duolingo. It never asks you to rate yourself when practising – you simply practice questions that have a clear right or wrong answer.

So **I pivoted the app** I was building from auto-flashcards as the core learning loop to auto-practice sessions. Now that this is done and live, I can tell you that this was a good decision. I personally prefer practising this way over flashcards, no matter how pretty and automated they were.

The rebuild here took **three months** while I was attending German classes. Three whole months! In hindsight that was absolutely a mistake. If i were to do it again I'd cut scope dramatically and ship the iOS app with just the core learning loop and nothing else, perhaps even without onboarding, to gauge if I was heading down the right path earlier.

[![image](./images/image-02.png)](./images/image-01.png)Demoing the 1-1 chat with a natural-sounding AI feature in Youtionary. It's wild how natural it feels now to talk to AI by voice

So here we are at present day – **my first iOS app ever went live on the App Store about three weeks ago. It's called Youtionary ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/youtionary-ai-language-tutor/id6756575480))** and I'm proud of what it is, what it can do, and what it'll be able to do in the coming weeks.

That's how my 7 months since quitting my job has gone. You guys are now up to speed!

---

But is there a point to telling us all this, Nick?

Well yes, of course. I mean, I hope so.

I hope that by detailing my story, you'd glimpse the messy process of ideation, testing, pivoting, and building a software business.

In 7 months of building, the biggest takeaway I've had is that an idea comes and an idea goes. **Execution determines everything. Execution leads to new things.**

I'm glad I didn't sit around planning for a month before doing anything. I jumped right in and made up the plan as I went. I'm still making it up... and I'm getting better at recognising which plan is hare-brained and which one might have a chance.

Youtionary is my first product idea turned real product. It has 50 free users, 1 paying user ($10 MRR), and what I think of as a bright future.

In a morning journaling session recently, I even had an epiphany of how to unify three disparate features into one, making it 10x more understandable to the average person, which should mean at least a few times more useful to most. I'm excited to get that update into the App Store some time this week.

Not to be morbid, but I might also kill the whole thing and move on to something else some time this year. It's a very real possibility! Whether I do that or not, I'm excited everyday by the fact that I have full autonomy to determine what I do and thereby where I'm headed.

Despite the downsides, this, my friends, is truly a fantastic way to live.
