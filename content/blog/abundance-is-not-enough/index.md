What the hell happened here?

Our mutual respect broke down in an unexpected instant and we devolved into a verbal battle. How could it happen so quickly, and more bafflingly, over something so trivial? What had gone wrong? Who had done wrong? How could we move on and learn from this?

---

I just raged at my 4 and a half year old daughter. My shout was like thunder that came from a lightning bolt splitting a tree in the backyard. Even I was surprised by it. I also slammed my hand so hard on the table that the cheap two-piece laminated chipboard table is now a little crooked.

All because of what?

Minutes before lightning struck, Charlotte told me that she'd scratched herself and that she was bleeding. It was a superficial wound less than half a centimeter wide. No biggie. But I knew she would want a plaster on it, so I go and grab one.

She then commanded me to put on the plaster a very particular way. The rainbow had to be like this, not like that. I peel off the wings of the plaster and hover over her wound. "Like this?"

"Ya," she approves. So I stick it on.

Five seconds later, her face is purple-red. She starts crying and complaining that I'd stuck it on incorrectly.

This has happened before. I give her time to realise it's not a big deal (which, so far, has not helped, but I do it anyway because I believe her ability to self-regulate her emotions may grown since the last time). But now she's not only crying, she's wailing. She's also blaming it on me.

So I point out calmly, "Charlotte, I asked if this was the way you wanted it, and you said 'yes', remember?"

She doesn't respond to this and I realise I may have lost her to some big feelings, because her crying is getting more and more dramatic.

My patience wears thin. What are we crying about again? The position of a plaster on the leg? Right... bite me.

A minute or so later (I don't know) I couldn't stand it anymore and said, "you told me this was the right position before I stuck it on. So if you're upset, you should blame yourself!"

"NO! I SHOULD NOT BLAME MYSELF, DADDY!" she screams at me, red face full of tears. "I SHOULD NOT BLAME MYSELF!!"

This is no ordinary yelling. It's the loudest I've heard her scream at me. So I lose it. I shouted back at her like thunder in her face and slammed the wooden table like lightning striking a tree.

"DON'T YOU RAISE YOUR VOICE AT ME!!"

She was completely taken aback and started crying even harder. She's now visibly shaken (so am I, my hands are literally shaking) and she's sobbing so violently that she's having a hard time breathing.

---

> "The priority of modern politics is economic growth. But humanity's struggle towards material security will only be worthwhile if we understand and find ways to attenuate the **psychological afflictions** that appear to continue into, and are sometimes directly fostered by, conditions of abundance."

This comes The School of Life by Alain de Botton. It's the passage that comes to mind as I'm wrestling with the incident.

He is talking about the afflictions of loneliness, anxiety, relationship breakdown, rage, humiliation, and depression. Supposedly _self-indulgent_ problems that make advanced societies have exceptionally high suicide rates.

A quarrel between father and daughter may be normal, but I feel an emotional knot having scared my child into (hopefully) learning a lesson about respect and responsibility.

Material abundance at home, which we have as a middle-class family, is not enough for a good life. We must respect and treat our psychological afflictions. What's scary to me is that our politics -- from Germany to Singapore -- truly are centered around material abundance (economic growth), so we're left to learn the right remedies ourselves without strong support.

That's hard.
