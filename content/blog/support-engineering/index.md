A selection of posts I've written about support engineering!

- [8 Hard Things about providing High Quality Customer Support](/2021-07-18-8-hard-things-about-providing-high-quality-customer-support/) — This captures what I learned from three years providing enterprise support, organising the operation, and building internal tools to help it scale.
- [Direct feedback](/2020-06-03-direct-feedback/) — This is an example of giving difficult feedback to protect support quality, customer commitments, and trust within the team.
- [9 Questions for 1-1s](/2021-09-12-9-useful-questions-for-1-1s/) — This explains how I use 1:1s to build trust, remove blockers, detect problems early, coach decisions, and help people grow.
- [Software bugs and trash](/2021-02-14-software-bugs-and-trash/) — I use an analogy to examine bug-intake friction, durable escalation workflows, automation, and the processing capacity needed to close the loop.
- [Be the person everyone wants to teach](/be-the-person-everyone-wants-to-teach/) — I make the case that private knowledge transfers should become reusable documentation, recordings, tools, or other assets that help the entire team.
- [What's one thing about coding that excites you?](/2020-03-09-whats-one-thing-about-coding-that-excites-you/) — This describes my former role as a combination of Technical Support Engineer, Solutions Engineer, and Internal Tools Engineer, using code to unblock customers and automate repetitive work.
- [Is Google Sheets robust enough for downloading data?](/2020-03-04-is-google-sheets-robust-enough-for-downloading-data/) — This walks through how I investigated logs to find the root cause of a third-party data failure affecting customers and considered engineering safeguards to prevent it from happening again.
- [Much more marketing topics on this blog](/2018-06-16-much-marketing-topics-blog/) — I argue here that excellent technical support requires understanding the customer's professional domain, not merely knowing how the product works.
- [How to deal with Daylight savings at work](/2019-04-11-how-to-deal-with-daylight-savings-at-work/) — This is a small example of my operational work staffing a 24/5 support chat across more than 15 global offices and preventing gaps in customer coverage.
- [To make a message stick, repeat it](/2021-01-24-to-make-a-message-stick-repeat-it/) — This shows how I defined and communicated my team's mission to reduce support demand by going upstream and fixing systemic problems.
- [What I learned from teaching colleagues how to code](/2020-05-07-what-i-learned-teaching-colleagues-how-to-code/) — This covers the two 13-week internal bootcamps I created to help customer-facing colleagues acquire the technical skills to solve more customer problems independently.
- [Devin added search on this blog](/devin-added-search-on-this-blog/) — This demonstrates how I scope work for coding agents, retain technical judgment, and use AI to make previously uneconomical engineering work practical.
- [How to lose trust fast](/how-to-lose-trust-fast/) — This reflection on one of my own mistakes explains why careful fact-finding and reading source material matter so much to organisational trust.

Together, these posts reflect how I think about Support Engineering: solve the immediate customer problem, then improve the product, knowledge, tooling, or team system that produced it.
